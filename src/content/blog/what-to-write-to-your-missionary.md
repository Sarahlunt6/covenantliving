When your missionary first leaves, the weekly email is the highlight of your week. By month six, you might find yourself staring at a blank screen, wondering what's left to say that you haven't said already.

The good news: your missionary doesn't need big news. They need to feel loved, remembered, and encouraged in the work. Here's how to write emails that do exactly that.

## What to include

- **Something you noticed in their letter.** Mention a person they're teaching by name, or ask a follow-up question. It shows you really read it.
- **A small, ordinary detail from home.** The dog stole a sandwich. The snow finally came. Ordinary news feels like home.
- **A scripture or thought from your own study.** Missionaries love knowing the family is studying too.
- **A specific way you're praying for them** or for the people they're teaching.
- **One line of pure encouragement.** "I'm so proud of you" never gets old.

## What to skip

- **Long lists of problems at home.** Share real life, but don't hand them worries they can't do anything about.
- **Pressure about what comes next.** School, jobs, and dating can wait until they're home.
- **"You're missing everything."** Focus on what they're doing, not what they're missing.

## Prompts for when you're stuck

Pick one or two each week:

1. What's one thing you learned about the Savior this week?
2. Tell me about your companion. What are they good at?
3. What's a meal you've had that I would never guess?
4. Who are you teaching that I can pray for by name?
5. What's a moment this week when you felt the Spirit?
6. What's something hard right now, and how can I help from here?
7. What's a word or phrase you've picked up in your mission?

## Keep a record for both of you

Missionary emails are easy to lose in an inbox. Take a few minutes after each one to write down what they shared, the names of people they're teaching, and how you're feeling. Over 18 or 24 months, those notes become a record of their mission and yours.

You'll also find it easier to plan packages and holidays ahead of time when you have one place to keep dates, addresses, and ideas.
