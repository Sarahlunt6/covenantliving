Missionary packages are a sweet way to say "we love you" from far away. They're also easy to send too late. A birthday package that arrives two weeks after the birthday still means a lot, but a little planning means it can arrive right on time.

Here's how to map out a whole year of packages in one sitting.

## Start with the occasions

Sit down with a calendar and list the moments you want to celebrate. Most families plan around some of these:

- **Arrival in the field** or the first weeks after the MTC
- **Birthday**
- **Easter**
- **Mother's Day and Father's Day**
- **Summer**, a mid-year "thinking of you" box
- **Halloween and Thanksgiving**
- **Christmas**, usually the biggest one of all

You don't need to send something for every occasion. Pick the ones that matter most to your family.

## Work backward from the arrival date

This is the most important step. For each occasion, write down three dates:

| Occasion | Should arrive by | Mail by | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Birthday | The birthday | Count back the shipping time | Your number |
| Christmas | Early December | Count back, plus extra holiday time | Your number |

Shipping takes longer than you think, especially for missionaries serving outside the country and during the holidays. Check the carrier's estimate, add a cushion, and set a reminder on your phone for the "mail by" date.

## Before you send anything

- **Confirm the address.** Many missions ask families to send packages to the mission office. Ask your missionary or the mission office where packages should go.
- **Check the mission's guidelines.** Some missions have rules about package size, frequency, or what can be sent.
- **For international missions,** look up the destination country's customs rules. Some items aren't allowed, and large packages can come with fees your missionary has to pay.

## What to send

**Things they'll actually use**
- Warm socks, gloves, or a good umbrella, depending on the climate
- Toiletries from home that are hard to find where they serve
- A new tie or scarf
- Study supplies: a nice pen, a small journal, scripture markers

**A taste of home**
- A favorite candy or snack that travels well
- A family recipe card for something they'll make when they get home

**Things that touch the heart**
- Letters from family members and friends
- Printed photos (they're easy to carry and easy to share)
- A short note for each day if it's a countdown package

**Something to share**
- A treat for their companion
- Small gifts for the people they're teaching or serving

## What to skip

- Heavy or bulky items that drive up postage
- Anything fragile
- Expensive electronics and valuables
- Anything they'd struggle to carry when they transfer
- Anything their mission doesn't allow (when in doubt, ask)

## Keep it all in one place

Write down every package you send: the date you mailed it, what was inside, and when it arrived. Next year, you'll know exactly how long shipping takes, and you won't send the same socks twice.
