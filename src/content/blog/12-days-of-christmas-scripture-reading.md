If an Advent calendar feels like too much, or you've already missed the first part of December, the twelve days before Christmas are a perfect place to start. This plan begins on December 13 and ends on Christmas Eve, and it follows a simple idea:

*Prophets foretold Him. Angels announced Him. A Savior was born.*

The first half of the plan reads the prophecies of Christ's birth, from Isaiah and the Book of Mormon. The second half reads how those prophecies were fulfilled.

## Why start with the prophets

It's easy to jump straight to the manger. But reading the prophecies first changes how the Christmas story feels. By the time you reach Luke 2, your family has spent a week watching prophets look forward to that night, some of them hundreds of years before it happened.

> For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.
>
> — Isaiah 9:6

## Don't skip day 11

The story in 3 Nephi 1 is one of the most powerful Christmas readings in all of scripture, and many families have never read it together. The believers in the Americas are about to be put to death if the sign of Christ's birth doesn't come. Nephi prays all day, and the Lord answers:

> Lift up your head and be of good cheer; for behold, the time is at hand, and on this night shall the sign be given, and on the morrow come I into the world…
>
> — 3 Nephi 1:13

Read it the night before Christmas Eve, and talk about what it means to trust the Lord's promises.

## Making it work with kids

- **Keep it short.** Some readings are long. With little ones, read a few key verses and summarize the rest.
- **Ask one question.** "Which name for Jesus in Isaiah 9:6 means the most to you?" is plenty.
- **Check off each day** on a chart they can see. Twelve checkmarks is a satisfying finish line.
- **Save Luke 2 for Christmas Eve** and read it by the tree with the lights low.

## Ending on Christmas Eve

On the last night, you'll read the words your family has been building toward all along:

> For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.
>
> — Luke 2:11

Twelve days, twelve readings, and a Christmas centered on the One it's all about.
