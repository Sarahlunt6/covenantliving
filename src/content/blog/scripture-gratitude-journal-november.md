November has a way of filling up fast. Between school, callings, and Thanksgiving plans, gratitude can turn into one rushed moment around the table. A few minutes with the scriptures each day changes that, and it's easier to keep up when you know exactly what to read and where to write it down.

Here's a simple plan for a scripture-centered November, whether you journal on your own or with your whole family.

## Why scripture-based gratitude works

The Lord doesn't ask for gratitude only on good days. In the Doctrine and Covenants He says:

> Thou shalt thank the Lord thy God in all things.
>
> — D&C 59:7

Writing down what we're thankful for, next to a verse that points us to the Savior, turns a nice habit into a spiritual one. Over a month, those small notes become a record of the Lord's hand in your life.

## A four-week plan

Pick one theme per week. Read the scripture on Sunday, then come back to it each day with a short prompt.

**Week 1: Thanksgiving as worship**

> Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.
>
> — Psalm 100:4

*Prompt:* Where did I feel close to Heavenly Father today?

**Week 2: Giving thanks in hard things**

> In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.
>
> — 1 Thessalonians 5:18

*Prompt:* What is one hard thing I can still find a blessing in?

**Week 3: Remembering to return and say thank you**

Only one of the ten lepers came back to thank Jesus (Luke 17:15–16).

*Prompt:* Who helped me this week, and how can I thank them?

**Week 4: Living in thanksgiving daily**

> ...live in thanksgiving daily, for the many mercies and blessings which he doth bestow upon you.
>
> — Alma 34:38

*Prompt:* What mercies did I notice today that I might have missed a month ago?

## Making it work for families

- **Keep it short.** One verse and one sentence each is plenty for little ones.
- **Read at dinner.** Read the week's scripture at dinner, and let each person share one thing before the meal.
- **Let kids draw.** A quick sketch counts as a journal entry.
- **Save the pages.** On Thanksgiving Day, read a few entries aloud before the meal.

## How to use the printable journal

1. Print the pages at home or at a print shop.
2. Clip or bind them, or keep them in a folder by the kitchen table.
3. Write or draw one entry each day.
4. Read your entries together on Thanksgiving.

> And he who receiveth all things with thankfulness shall be made glorious.
>
> — D&C 78:19
