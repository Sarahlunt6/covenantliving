The Articles of Faith are thirteen short statements of what we believe. The Prophet Joseph Smith wrote them in 1842, and they're still one of the clearest ways to explain the restored gospel of Jesus Christ. They're also wonderful for children to memorize. Once those words are in a child's heart, they stay there.

Thirteen can feel like a lot, though. The trick is to go slowly, make it visual, and make it fun.

## Start with the first one

The first Article of Faith is the foundation for all the others:

> We believe in God, the Eternal Father, and in His Son, Jesus Christ, and in the Holy Ghost.
>
> — Articles of Faith 1:1

It's short, it's clear, and even preschoolers can learn it in a week or two. Celebrate when they do. That first success makes the rest feel possible.

## One article at a time

Pick a pace that fits your family. Here are two that work well:

| Pace | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One a month | Thirteen months to learn them all, with lots of review | Younger children, busy families |
| One every two weeks | About six months, with a review week every so often | Older children, Primary classes |

Whatever pace you choose, keep reviewing the ones they already know. A quick run-through of the earlier articles before starting a new one keeps them fresh.

## Make it visual

Children remember what they can see. A picture for each article gives them a hook to hang the words on: a family kneeling for faith, a baptism for the fourth article, the Bible and the Book of Mormon for the eighth.

- **Hang the current card** on the fridge or bathroom mirror where they'll see it every day.
- **Put the learned cards on a ring** so they can flip through them.
- **Use the picture as a prompt.** Show the card and let them say the words.

## Games that help it stick

Short and playful beats long and serious every time:

1. **Missing word.** Say the article and leave out one word for them to fill in. Leave out more each day.
2. **Echo.** Say a phrase, and they repeat it back. Then switch who leads.
3. **Toss and say.** Pass a ball or beanbag around. Each person says the next few words.
4. **Scramble.** Write each phrase on a strip of paper, mix them up, and let them put the article back in order.
5. **Teach it back.** Have them explain what the article means in their own words. Understanding helps memory.

## A few minutes a day

You don't need a long lesson. Two or three minutes at breakfast, in the car, or at bedtime is plenty. Consistency matters more than length.

## Talk about what it means

Memorizing is a great start, but the goal is testimony. When you reach the thirteenth article, take time to talk about what it looks like to live it:

> We believe in being honest, true, chaste, benevolent, virtuous, and in doing good to all men…
>
> — Articles of Faith 1:13

Ask, "When did you see someone being honest this week? How can we do good to someone tomorrow?" That's when the words start becoming part of who they are.
