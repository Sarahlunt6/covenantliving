Some of the sweetest Christmas memories happen in a living room, with a bathrobe for a shepherd's cloak and a baby doll in a laundry basket. Acting out the Nativity on Christmas Eve takes almost no preparation, and it puts the story of the Savior's birth right in the middle of your celebration.

The secret is to use the scriptures themselves. When a narrator reads Luke 2 word for word and everyone else acts it out, even the littlest ones are hearing the real story.

## Why read it word for word

Paraphrased versions are cute, but the words of Luke 2 have a power of their own. Children who act out "Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy" year after year end up knowing those verses by heart. That's a gift that lasts long after the costumes are put away.

> Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.
>
> — Luke 2:14

## Who plays whom

You can do this with three people or thirty. Here are the parts, from most important to optional:

- **Narrator:** a parent or older child who reads the verses
- **Mary and Joseph**
- **The Angel:** stands on a step stool if you have one
- **Shepherds:** as many as you like
- **Heavenly Host:** everyone not on stage joins in on verse 14
- **Innkeeper:** a great part for a child who wants a line without much pressure
- **Sheep:** perfect for toddlers, who mostly need to crawl and say "baa"
- **Wise Men:** optional, using Matthew 2

Not enough people? Stuffed animals make excellent sheep, and one person can play more than one part.

## Costumes from around the house

You don't need to buy anything. Raid the linen closet:

- **Mary:** a blue or light-colored sheet draped over the head
- **Joseph:** a bathrobe tied with a belt, a dish towel on the head
- **Shepherds:** bath towels, striped dish towels, and a cane or broom handle for a staff
- **Angel:** a white pillowcase or T-shirt and a halo of gold tinsel or a pipe cleaner
- **Wise Men:** fancy robes or blankets as capes, paper crowns, and gifts made from small boxes

## The scenes

Read the verses in order and let each group act out its part. Here's a simple outline:

| Scene | Read | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The journey | Luke 2:1–5 | Mary and Joseph walk slowly around the room |
| No room | Luke 2:6–7 | The innkeeper shakes his head; Mary lays the baby in the manger |
| The shepherds | Luke 2:8–14 | Dim the lights; the angel appears; everyone says verse 14 together |
| Let us now go | Luke 2:15–20 | The shepherds hurry to the manger and kneel |
| The wise men | Matthew 2:1–11 | Optional: the wise men follow a flashlight star and bring their gifts |

The whole thing takes about 15 minutes, which is just right for little attention spans.

## Tips for a calm, sweet evening

1. **Do one quick walk-through** before dinner so everyone knows where to stand.
2. **Let kids pick their parts** a few days ahead. They'll be excited all week.
3. **Turn off the overhead lights** for the shepherds scene and use a flashlight for the angel's glory.
4. **End with a song.** "Silent Night" or "Away in a Manger" sung around the manger is a perfect finale.
5. **Take a family photo in costume**, and write down who played whom. In ten years, you'll treasure that list.

## Make it a tradition

The first year might be a little chaotic. That's fine. By the third or fourth Christmas Eve, your kids will be asking for their favorite parts and saying the lines along with the narrator. That's the moment you'll know the story has found a home in their hearts.
